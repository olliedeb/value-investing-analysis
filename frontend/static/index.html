History — rebuild the past

  
Reconstructs the ranking as it stood on each past rebalance date, from the 10-K filings that were public by then and the price on the day — then saves those rankings as snapshots so the backtest can run over years, not just since today. It is resumable: stop it any time and it picks up where it left off. First run takes a while (it downloads years of prices for ~600 tickers); re-runs are fast because everything is cached. Snapshot frequency is your choice: quarterly is the default, and a longer interval (yearly, every 5 years) is much faster since there are fewer dates to rebuild — just keep it at least as frequent as the holding period you plan to test, because the backtest reuses the latest snapshot on each rebalance date. See portfolio/README.md for the method and its limits (survivorship, annual EPS for P/E).

Simulate returns

  
Pick a period and how long each position is held — 1 month, 3 months, 1 year, 2 years, 3 years, 5 years, or buy and hold — then simulate. On each rebalance date it buys the top-ranked stocks from the ranking as it stood on that date — never today's ranking replayed over the past (that would be trading on information that didn't exist yet). The rebalance history below shows exactly what was held at each rebalance, and which snapshot it came from. It uses the weighting from the portfolio above. It needs at least two snapshot dates — if there isn't enough history yet, it will say so instead of inventing a past it never saw. Prices are cached under portfolio/data/prices/.
